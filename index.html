What I bring to an ML team

Problems without a playbook

Infant fingerprints change shape as children grow, images come from low-cost phones in bright sun, and there is no public dataset to start from. I scope work like this into experiments a team can actually run, with clear go/no-go evidence for funders.

Evaluation that reflects reality

I set up training, benchmarking and evaluation around data collected in the populations a model will serve, so accuracy numbers mean something when the model reaches a health clinic thousands of miles from where it was trained.

Privacy as a design constraint

Biometrics for vulnerable people raises the bar on data protection. I've led template-protection research with the ICRC and shipped it as open-source code others can audit and reuse.

From model to product

Eight years of product management across startups and scale-ups means I care whether a model ships, what it costs to run, and whether frontline health workers can use it.

Contactless biometrics for infants

Babies are the people most likely to be missed by health records, and the hardest to identify: their fingers are tiny, soft and growing. I lead the initiative to develop the first reliable contactless infant biometric recognition algorithm, from data-collection design through model training and evaluation.

  • Leading a cross-functional team of ML engineers and data scientists on model training, benchmarking and evaluation.
  • Designing a field data-collection effort targeting over 4 million biometric images, with clinical-research standards (GCP-trained).
  • Translating research progress into milestones and evidence for funders and partners.

SimFace: on-device face recognition

Launched SimFace, an open-source Android library for face detection, quality assessment, embedding and matching on low-cost mobile devices. It builds on the EdgeFace architecture and ships with SimQ, a configurable face-quality scorer, so poor captures are caught at the point of enrolment rather than in the data.

Protecting biometric templates in humanitarian systems

Led a research collaboration with the International Committee of the Red Cross to choose and evaluate a biometric template protection scheme for mobile humanitarian deployments. We selected PolyProtect and tested it on real face data from a humanitarian project in Ethiopia, measuring accuracy, irreversibility and unlinkability, and published the first evaluation of PolyProtect for identification and for fingerprints.
