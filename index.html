How I lead ML teams

Problems without a playbook

There is no public dataset of infant biometrics, least of all from low- and middle-income countries, and an infant's features change as they grow. I break problems like this into experiments a small team can run, and keep everyone focused on the one number that matters.

Evaluation that reflects reality

I set up training, benchmarking and evaluation around data collected from the people a model will serve, tracked over time, so accuracy numbers still hold when the model reaches a clinic in rural Ghana or South Sudan.

Privacy as a design constraint

Biometrics for vulnerable people raises the bar on data protection. I've led template-protection research with the ICRC and shipped it as open-source code others can audit and reuse.

Built for the edge, not the cloud

Our models run fully offline on low-spec Android phones, using only the phone camera. I keep the team designing for that from day one, and bring a product background that asks whether a model ships, what it costs to run, and whether a frontline health worker can use it.

Contactless biometrics for infants

Babies are the people most likely to be missed by health records, and the hardest to identify. I lead a $6m initiative to develop the first reliable contactless infant biometric recognition algorithm, from data collection through model training, evaluation and deployment.

Three things make it hard:

  • No data to start from. There are no public datasets of infant biometrics, especially from low- and middle-income countries.
  • The subject keeps changing. Infants grow fast, so a model has to recognise the same child months later.
  • Edge only. Capture and matching run fully contactless on a low-spec Android phone, with no cloud and only the phone camera.

Leading a team of two ML engineers, a backend engineer and, earlier in the programme, a data scientist, we built a longitudinal dataset of over 4 million images from a cohort of 13,000 infants in Ghana and Bangladesh, captured monthly for 12 months, and raised the true match rate at 1% false match rate from around 40% to around 90% over a three-month growth period.

SimFace: on-device face recognition

Launched SimFace, an open-source Android library for face detection, quality assessment, embedding and matching on low-cost mobile devices. It builds on the EdgeFace architecture and ships with SimQ, a configurable face-quality scorer, so poor captures are caught at the point of enrolment rather than in the data.

Protecting biometric templates in humanitarian systems

Led a research collaboration with the International Committee of the Red Cross to choose and evaluate a biometric template protection scheme for mobile humanitarian deployments. We selected PolyProtect and tested it on real face data from a humanitarian project in Ethiopia, measuring accuracy, irreversibility and unlinkability, and published the first evaluation of PolyProtect for identification and for fingerprints.
